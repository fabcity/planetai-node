# The licence, decided on purpose

**Raised 18 September 2026 by the design review. Drafted 20 September. Decided 25 September: row (B),
AGPL-3.0-or-later, see [2026-09-25-licence.md](2026-09-25-licence.md). `LICENSE` stays untouched until every
contributor has consented.**

The code is Apache-2.0 (`LICENSE`), docs CC-BY 4.0 (`CONTRIBUTING.md`). Neither was chosen against an
alternative. The design review reports that the April core-ideas paper promised Apache-2.0 / CERN-OHL
and OSHWA certification for hardware — that paper is not in this repository and was not read for this
record. What is checkable here: no hardware repository exists yet.

The programme's thesis is sovereignty by architecture: raw readings never leave the box
(`ARCHITECTURE.md §7`). A permissive licence is the one thing that lets a vendor ship "PLANETAI Cloud"
that federates raw readings to their servers, legally, with the name on it. **That may be an acceptable
trade. It has not been decided; it was the default.**

---

## §1 · What the stack already is, checked rather than assumed

The review pointed at a models memo for the copyleft in the dependency tree. That memo is not in this
repository. What follows was read off `app/requirements.txt`, the seventeen `packs/*/pack.yaml` files
and PyPI on 20 September 2026, which is better evidence anyway: these are shipping today, not proposed.

**Strong copyleft is already here.**

| package | licence | where |
|---|---|---|
| `python-miio` | **GPL-3.0-only** | `packs/xiaomi-air` — `pip:` line, verified live on node #1 |
| `psycopg` | **LGPL-3.0-only** | `app/requirements.txt` — every node, the database driver |

Everything else is permissive: `fastapi` MIT, `uvicorn` BSD-3, `httpx` BSD-3, `pyyaml` MIT, `mcp` MIT,
`h3` Apache-2.0, `rasterio` BSD-3, `numpy` BSD-3-and-friends, `earthengine-api` Apache-2.0,
`paho-mqtt` EPL-2.0-or-BSD-3, `cryptography` Apache-2.0-or-BSD-3.

Two consequences, and the first is a live question rather than a future one:

- **An Apache-2.0 core already loads a GPL-3.0 pack in its own process.** `packs/xiaomi-air` declares
  `pip: python-miio`, the adapter runs inside the app container under `PACKS_ALLOW_CODE=1`, and
  `make ship` conveys the whole tree in a tarball. Whether that is a combined work is a question for
  counsel; what is certain is that **nobody has written it down**, and option (A) — stay permissive —
  is the option under which it stays awkward. Under (B) or (C) it simply resolves: AGPL-3.0 and
  GPL-3.0 combine.
- **Apache-2.0 → GPL/AGPL-3.0 is one-way.** Apache-2.0 code can be taken into an AGPL-3.0 work; the
  reverse is not true. So every permissive upstream above is compatible with (B) and (C) without
  anyone's permission. The only consent needed is from this project's own contributors — §5.

**Data share-alike is already here too, and it is load-bearing.** `packs/place` stores OpenStreetMap
geometry — **ODbL**, a share-alike data licence — in the node's own `place_features` table, and
`GET /place/geojson` serves derivatives of it. The pack attributes correctly today. But ODbL is a
copyleft on *data*, and the node is redistributing a Produced Work from it, which means the "what
licence are packs under" question in §4 is not hypothetical: one shipped pack is already carrying an
obligation that no file in this repository states.

**Seventeen packs, zero declared licences.** Not one `packs/*/pack.yaml` has a `licence:` key, and
`docs/PACKS.md` does not contain the word. A pack is explicitly third-party code a node loads. §4.

## §2 · The three options, each by its best advocate

### (A) Stay Apache-2.0, and put the sovereignty protection in a trademark policy

*Argued by its advocate:*

> The licence is the wrong instrument and you are reaching for it because it is the one you know.
> Nobody adopts a node because of its licence; they adopt it because a fab lab can install it in an
> afternoon and a university's legal department does not have to be asked. AGPL is the single most
> reliable way to make an institutional partner's counsel say no — not because it is bad, but because
> it takes six weeks to get an answer and the answer is often no by default.
>
> What you actually want to prevent is somebody shipping a thing *called PLANETAI* that federates raw
> readings. That is a **naming** problem and a trademark solves it exactly. The conformance test is
> already written: `ARCHITECTURE.md §7` is five refusals, and most of them are mechanically checkable.
> Publish a policy — a node may call itself a PLANETAI node when it passes the conformance suite — and
> you have protected the thing you care about at the point where it does damage, which is the claim
> made to a household, not the code on a server.
>
> And be honest about what AGPL buys you against a competent adversary: they publish their source,
> comply, and still run the cloud. AGPL compels disclosure; it does not compel architecture.

**Cost:** protects the name, not the code. A vendor may legally ship the federating product under a
different name. Leaves the GPL-3.0 pack question in §1 unresolved.

### (B) AGPL-3.0 for the node

*Argued by its advocate:*

> You wrote down a sovereignty claim and then chose the one licence family that cannot defend it at the
> boundary where it is attacked. The attack is not "somebody forks the code"; it is "somebody runs it
> as a service and the household's readings end up on their disk". **AGPL-3.0 §13 is the only clause in
> mainstream licensing that reaches a network boundary**, and this project's whole architecture is a
> network boundary. Apache-2.0 protects a distributor who never distributes.
>
> The trademark argument in (A) is not an alternative, it is a *complement*, and you should do it
> either way. But a trademark policy protects a word. AGPL protects the households whose data is on
> the other end of somebody's federation, including the ones who never heard the word.
>
> The compatibility cost people fear is already paid: a GPL-3.0 pack is in the tree today and the
> dependency tree is otherwise permissive, so every upstream is one-way compatible with AGPL. The cost
> that is real is institutional, and it is smaller than it looks: a university *using* a node is not
> distributing or conveying anything, so §13 never fires for them. Their counsel's objection is usually
> to contributing, not to running.

**Cost:** some institutional partners' legal departments. Every pack must be AGPL-compatible — which
today means **every pack must declare a licence at all**, and none does (§4). A vendor wanting a
permissive core for an internal product is pushed to a fork or to an exception licence sold by the
Foundation, which is a business decision this record does not make.

### (C) Apache core, AGPL federation

*Argued by its advocate:*

> The two previous advocates are both arguing about the wrong unit. Look at what the code actually
> does. `app/sources.py`, the rules engine, the report writer and the dashboard are a household
> appliance; nothing about them threatens anybody's sovereignty, and making them AGPL buys nothing and
> costs adoption. The parts that carry the risk are precisely the parts that move data between
> machines: `push_aggregates`, `push_events`, `receive_aggregates`, `receive_events`, `app/index.py`
> and the Index publisher. That is a few hundred lines and it is where "PLANETAI Cloud" would have to
> touch.
>
> So licence the household permissively — every fab lab, every university, every hobbyist, no
> conversation with counsel — and put AGPL-3.0 on the federation and publishing code. A vendor can
> build a household product on the permissive part and is welcome to. The moment they federate, §13
> fires, and that is the exact moment anybody should care.

**Cost:** hardest to explain, and an explanation nobody can hold in their head is a licence nobody
complies with. Needs a per-file or per-directory boundary that stays true as the code moves — and this
repository moves code between files constantly (`app/issues/` was carved out of `main.py` this month).
A boundary that drifts is worse than either pure option. Mitigation: put the AGPL half in its own
directory with its own `LICENSE`, and make it a lint gate, the way `tools/check_wire.py` holds the wire
contract. That is real work, not a header comment.

## §3 · What each option changes, for four readers

### A fab lab running a node

**Nothing, under any of the three. Say this first and say it plainly**, because it is the question
most people are actually asking and the answer is the same in every column. Running the software is
not conveying it. No obligation of any kind attaches to installing a node, operating it, modifying it
for yourself, or showing a household their own page. AGPL-3.0 §13 fires on *offering it to others over
a network*, which a fab lab running its own node does not do.

### A company selling the Kept Alive tier

**This is the real question**, and `PRODUCT.md`'s "Kept alive — this is the business. margin lives
here" is why.

| | (A) Apache | (B) AGPL | (C) split |
|---|---|---|---|
| install and maintain nodes for paying households | unaffected | unaffected — the node is the household's, not offered to them over a network | unaffected |
| run a dashboard **on the company's servers** showing a customer's data | unaffected | **§13 fires**: source to those users | **§13 fires** |
| aggregate customers into a district view they log into | unaffected | **§13 fires** | **§13 fires** |
| ship a closed product built on this code | unaffected | not without the Foundation's permission | unaffected for the household half |

Under (B) and (C) the Kept Alive tier as `PRODUCT.md` describes it — alerts, a monthly brief, remote
monitoring, a sensor swap — is **unaffected**, because remote monitoring by the operator is not
offering the program to the household over a network. What changes is the day somebody wants a hosted
multi-tenant dashboard. That is the product decision hiding inside the licence decision, and it should
be made by whoever intends to sell it.

### A partner university

Using it: unaffected in all three. Contributing: (B) and (C) make some tech-transfer offices slower,
and a few will decline. Publishing a paper from node data: unaffected — the data is CC-BY 4.0 in the
export and always was.

### A fork

(A) anybody, any purpose, no obligation. (B) may fork and must publish changes when offering over a
network; may not close it. (C) the household half forks freely, the federation half does not.

## §4 · Packs, which need a decision whichever row is struck

**Recommend, and this is independent of §6:**

- **Pack data — `rules.yml`, `cells.yml`, `channels.yml`, thresholds, messages — is CC-BY-4.0.** It is
  a claim about a place with a source behind it, which is a document, not a program. This matches the
  export's licence and the sources registry.
- **Pack code — `adapter.py` and anything in `pip:` — follows the core**, whatever §6 chooses.
- **`pack.yaml` gains a required `licence:` key**, and `tools/check_registry.py` fails without it.
  Today seventeen packs declare nothing and `docs/PACKS.md` never says the word, so a third-party pack
  ships with no terms at all. That is the gap to close first, before it is seventeen plus a network's
  worth.
- **`docs/PACKS.md` states both**, next to the `PACKS_ALLOW_CODE` warning, which is where a pack author
  is already being asked to think about what a pack is allowed to do.
- **Data a pack redistributes keeps its own terms**, as `NOTICE` already says for sources. `packs/place`
  is the worked example and the warning: ODbL is share-alike on data, and `/place/geojson` serves a
  Produced Work from it.

**If (B) is chosen, check this before announcing it:** `packs/xiaomi-air` depends on GPL-3.0 code.
AGPL-3.0 and GPL-3.0 are compatible in the direction that matters, so this is fine — but it should be
*verified* rather than assumed, and it is the one pack where the answer is not trivially yes.

## §5 · Re-licensing mechanics, if (B) or (C)

Every contributor to date, counted on 20 September 2026:

| | commits |
|---|---|
| Tomas Diez (five author identities) | 431 |
| Lucas Marangoni (two identities) | 2 |
| authored by an AI | 8 |
| **total, excluding merges** | **441** |

**A one-paragraph written consent from Lucas is the whole procedure.** That is the finding: the
re-licensing cost is one email, today. `CONTRIBUTING.md` already says "Code Apache 2.0, docs CC-BY 4.0.
By opening a PR you agree to those terms" — which grants inbound rights under Apache-2.0 and does not
grant the right to relicense, so consent is genuinely needed and genuinely cheap.

**It gets harder every month, and the review's framing is right:** the first external pack author, the
first university PR, the first fab lab that sends a fix — each one adds a person who must be found
years later. There is no CLA here and this record does not propose one; a DCO sign-off is proposed in
PR #96 and a DCO does not grant relicensing rights either. **If (B) or (C) is even plausible, do it
before the contributor list grows**, or adopt an inbound licence that permits it, which is its own
decision and a less popular one.

**The AI's eight commits are a question for counsel, not for this record.** Purely machine-generated
output may have no copyright holder at all, in which case there is nothing to consent to; the 331
commits carrying a `Co-Authored-By` trailer had a human directing them and the trailer is a courtesy
attribution, not a copyright claim. This record flags it rather than resolving it. **Do not let it
become the reason nothing is decided** — the practical consent set is Tomas and Lucas.

## §6 · The recommendation, which Tomas can strike

**Recommended: (B) AGPL-3.0 for the node, plus the trademark policy from (A).**

Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **The sovereignty claim is the product.** `ARCHITECTURE.md §7` says no raw readings leave the
   instance that recorded them, and `PRODUCT.md` sells that. Apache-2.0 makes that claim true of *this*
   code and untrue of anything built from it, and the failure mode — a vendor federating households'
   readings, legally — is the exact thing the architecture exists to prevent.
2. **It is cheapest today and never gets cheaper.** One email to Lucas. The dependency tree is already
   one-way compatible. A GPL-3.0 pack already ships. Every month this is deferred, the consent set
   grows.
3. **A fab lab notices nothing.** §3's first row is the same in all three columns, and that is the
   population this project is for.

**(A) is the honest second choice** and its advocate's strongest point stands: AGPL compels disclosure,
not architecture. Take the trademark policy from it regardless of which row survives — that work is
needed in all three, and `ARCHITECTURE.md §7`'s refusals are already the conformance test.

**(C) is rejected not on its merits but on its boundary.** It is the most precisely-aimed option and
the one this repository is least able to hold: `app/issues/` was carved out of `main.py` this month, and
a licence boundary that drifts with a refactor is worse than either pure answer. It becomes viable the
day the federation code has its own directory and a gate that keeps it there.

### Hardware

Not in this repository and not blocked by it. When the enclosure work moves out of the outdoor-sensor
design and into a repository of its own:

**CERN-OHL-S** (strongly reciprocal) to match a copyleft core under (B), or **CERN-OHL-W** (weakly
reciprocal) if a partner needs to combine our enclosure with their own proprietary mechanics. **-P**
(permissive) only if the decision below is (A). Recommend **-S**, with OSHWA certification as the April
paper promised, in a separate repository under the same Foundation account — a hardware licence in a
software repository confuses every reader of both.

### The trademark position

> **BLANK — for Tomas.** The Fab City Foundation's existing trademark position on "Fab City" and on
> "PLANETAI" is not stated here, because this record's author does not know it and will not guess.
> What is needed before the trademark half of the recommendation can be written: what is registered,
> in which jurisdictions, by which entity, and whether "PLANETAI" is registered at all or is only a
> programme name. `PRODUCT.md` says the household-facing name is *"not decided; goes through the Fab
> City brand rules when it is"*, which suggests the rules exist and this record has not read them.

## §7 · What this record does not do

It does not edit `LICENSE` or `NOTICE`. It does not change `CONTRIBUTING.md`'s inbound terms. It does
not add a `licence:` key to any pack — §4 is a recommendation and lands in its own PR. It does not
state the Foundation's trademark position. And it decides nothing: a row in §6 is struck by a person,
and the strike is what makes this a decision rather than a memo.
